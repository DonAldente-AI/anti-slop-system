# Getting Started in the Kitchen (Without Losing Your Mind)

Cooking intimidation is totally normal — kitchens can feel like alien laboratories when you're starting out. The good news? You don't need to become Gordon Ramsay overnight. Here's how to ease in:

## Start Stupidly Simple

**Week 1-2: Master these basics:**
- Scrambled eggs
- Pasta with butter and parmesan  
- Grilled cheese sandwich
- Basic salad with store-bought dressing

These teach fundamental skills (heat control, timing, seasoning) without stress.

## Essential Equipment (Not the Whole Store)

You need way less than you think:
- One good knife (chef's knife, 8-inch)
- Cutting board
- Non-stick pan
- Medium pot
- Wooden spoon

That's it. Seriously. Everything else can wait.

## Redraft (2/10 Slop)

You know what? Fuck the motivational speeches. I burned my first omelet so badly it looked like a hockey puck, and I ate it anyway because I was poor and stubborn. That was fifteen years ago, and I still remember the exact bitter taste of char mixed with disappointment.

Here's what I wish someone had told me instead of all that "cooking is an adventure!" garbage:

Start with pasta. Not because it's "easy" – because it's cheap when you screw up. Buy the blue box Barilla (not the fancy bronze-die stuff yet), fill a pot with water, add salt until it tastes like the ocean. Actually taste it. The water should be aggressively salty. Boil the hell out of it, then dump in the pasta. Set a timer for exactly one minute less than the box says. That's it. You now know how to not make sad, mushy noodles.

I used to think "al dente" was pretentious nonsense until I realized mushy pasta feels like eating library paste. The difference is real, and it's about 60 seconds of cooking time.

For your first real meal, make spaghetti aglio e olio. It's just pasta, garlic, olive oil, and pasta water. Sounds simple, right? I fucked this up seventeen times. The garlic burned (bitter), the oil separated (looked like a crime scene), the pasta was either crunchy or mushy (never just right). But it taught me everything: how hot pans get, how fast garlic goes from golden to ruined, why pasta water is magical starchy glue.

Here's the actual technique: Thin-slice 4-5 garlic cloves. Start them in a cold pan with olive oil – maybe 1/3 cup for a pound of pasta. Turn the heat to medium-low. The garlic should bubble gently, not aggressively. You want golden edges, not brown. If it's browning fast, pull the pan off the heat entirely. This took me forever to learn because I'm impatient and kept cranking the heat.
